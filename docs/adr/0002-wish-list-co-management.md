# Separate wish-list co-management from recipient identity

A self-managed account holder can authorize one or more wish-list co-managers through mutual consent while retaining their account. An accepted grant is independent of the connection that made the users eligible and lasts until either person ends it. Keep this authority separate from managed-profile control and list ownership: those concepts confer broader profile powers or identify gift recipients. Co-managers act under their own identities, retain normal gift-giver abilities, and never inherit the account holder's login, friendships, or private gift planning.

Each grant covers all current and future lists where the account holder is the only recipient and no other recipient is pending, including unshared lists. Excluding joint and pending-joint lists prevents one person's consent from delegating authority over another person's wishes. Every co-manager can maintain wishes and configure eligible personal lists regardless of who created them, including sharing and permitted policy changes, so the account holder can remain hands off. Recipient changes, co-manager administration, and permanent deletion remain with the account holder. Event-list settings and lifecycle retain their existing event authority.

Delegated sharing uses the account holder's eligible audiences. Reveal only the group information needed to select and understand an audience; this grants neither membership nor access to other group content. Using the helper's memberships instead would expose wishes to audiences outside the account holder's sharing authority and prevent helpers from maintaining the account holder's existing audiences.

First co-manager access triggers the same permanent privacy-policy lock as first group sharing. The helper can shop from an otherwise unshared list, so waiting for group sharing would allow a later policy change to reveal their gifts. Initial policy can be selected when creating a list; subsequent changes obey existing privacy locks, which remain after co-management ends. Acting-user attribution and activity must preserve recipient surprise protections.

Published wishes and completed edits stay with the account holder when delegated access ends. Personal drafts remain private to their authors, who retain recoverable copies when a grant ends or a list leaves scope; authority over a destination does not transfer ownership of another person's unfinished work. Independent viewing permissions determine whether existing gift commitments remain or follow normal access-loss rules. Claiming a managed profile ends its old management arrangements and requires fresh consent for this narrower role.

The feature specification and acceptance criteria are tracked in [issue #221](https://github.com/Affolder-Creative/little-hint/issues/221).
